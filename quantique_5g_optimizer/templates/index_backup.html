Quantum Approach: QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm)

How Hybrid Quantum-Classical Computing Searches the Non-Convex 5G Solution Space

QAOA Explained From Zero

QAOA stands for Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm. It is a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm designed for noisy, intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) computers.

Here is how it works in simple language:

  1. Convert to Math: The 5G resource allocation problem is converted into a mathematical optimization problem called a QUBO (Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization) or Ising cost Hamiltonian.
  2. Encode into Qubits: Each binary decision variable (e.g., whether Tower 1 selects candidate configuration 0) is mapped to one quantum bit (qubit). Our prototype uses 12 qubits.
  3. Quantum Circuit Search: Parameterized quantum gates ($U(C, \gamma)$ cost unitary and $U(B, \beta)$ mixer unitary) explore the 4,096-state Hilbert space. Quantum tunneling allows the algorithm to tunnel through the 18.5 dB energy barrier where classical greedy search gets trapped.
  4. Classical Optimizer Updates: A classical optimizer (COBYLA) iteratively tunes the quantum circuit angles over 25 cycles to minimize the cost function.
  5. Global Ground State: QAOA outputs the best feasible state: |100001000001⟩ (initial cost +42.5 drops to optimal cost -18.8).
Realistic Engineering Clarification: We do NOT claim that QAOA magically "tries every solution simultaneously" or offers infinite speedup. In this prototype, QAOA acts as an advanced combinatorial search coprocessor that works alongside a classical optimizer (Quantum circuit + Classical optimizer = Hybrid Quantum Optimization) to find high-quality configurations within the O-RAN <10ms execution budget.
